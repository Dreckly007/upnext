Player & Organizer Guide

How to run a session, a ladder, or a tournament in Up Next

Three ways to get a group of players onto the court, fairly. Pick the one that matches what you're actually doing tonight.

Playing once

Session Scheduler

A one-off get-together. Everyone rotates through fair, varied matchups for the night, then it's done.

Playing for weeks

Ladder

An ongoing group. Players build up a rating over many sessions, or quick matches logged on the spot.

One event

Tournament

A competitive day: groups, a knockout bracket, and a champion crowned at the end.

Getting started

The Home screen is where every session, ladder, and tournament begins.

Tap a mode card to start. Above them is the Colour theme dropdown. At the bottom, paste a share code or link someone sent you to view their schedule.

Colour theme

Choose from 15 ready-made themes, or build your own. Tap Custom and you'll get two colour pickers: Main colour sets the accent used on buttons and headings, Background colour sets the page itself. Pick a dark background and the app works out readable text and borders automatically — no need to tune anything else by hand.

Session Scheduler

For one night: add who's playing, and the app handles fair rotation from there.

Set up a session

  1. Add players — type a name, or tap one from your saved list so you never retype a regular.
  2. Match format — Doubles or Singles, and how many courts you've got.
  3. How long to play — set a number of games or a time budget, plus the scoring (points to win, win by two).

Play a round

Each round shows a card per court with both teams on it, and a Resting list below for anyone sitting out that round. Enter scores right on the card, then Next → to move on. Behind the scenes, the app is spreading out who sits out, who partners whom, and who plays whom — so the same faces don't keep ending up together.

Manage players mid-session

Open Manage Players for everything roster-related: Start Over, Edit Players, Add Player, Remove Player, Swap Positions, and Link Players.

  • Add Player — choose New player to type a fresh name, or Saved player to tap one from your list.
  • Remove Player / Swap Positions — tap who to remove, or tap two players to swap where they're playing.
  • Any change asks whether it applies to this game only, this game and all remaining games, or to update the remaining schedule from here on.

Link Players has its own section below — it works a little differently from the rest.

Session Tools

  • Timer — start and pause the round clock, or Set time to adjust it mid-round (handy if you swap ends partway through). Full screen gives you a big, courtside display.
  • Regenerate Schedule — reshuffle the rounds still to come, if something about the current plan isn't working.
  • Criteria — a plain-language fairness report for the night.
Reading the Criteria line. It covers how many rounds everyone actually got to play on average, the worst-case repeat for any partner pair, any opponent pair, and any group of four sharing a court, plus how many times a partner became an immediate opponent the very next round. Lower numbers across the board mean a fairer night — the app is already trying to minimise all of them when it builds the schedule.

Linking & unlinking players

Keep two specific people playing together, instead of rotating them apart.

Why link two players. Real-life partners, siblings, a coach playing with a student, or simply balancing a stronger player against a weaker one as a single team — any time you want two people to stay a fixed doubles pair all night, instead of rotating through new partners like everyone else.

In a Session

  • Before you generate: on the setup screen, use Pair up players — tap two names to lock them together.
  • Mid-session: open Manage Players → Link Players, then tap two players on a court (or in the resting list) to pair them. Tap an already-linked player to unlink them, with a confirmation first.
  • Linked players carry a small 🔗 mark wherever their name appears, including while resting.

In a Ladder

Only available when that ladder's round-robin tracking is switched off. When it's on, the whole point is steering everyone toward partnering everyone else eventually — so locking two people together would work against it, and the option is hidden.

In a Tournament

There's no linking step at all — you enter a doubles team as both players together, right from the start, so they're never split up in the first place.

Ladder

An ongoing group that tracks a rating for every player, session after session.

Start a ladder

Give it a name, choose Doubles or Singles, and decide on Track full round-robin:

  • Yes (the default) — steers every new session toward partners and opponents nobody's had yet, aiming for everyone to eventually play everyone else.
  • No — runs it as an open-ended ladder. Ratings only, no target to work toward.

Adding and removing players

Players join through the same Players step as a normal session — anyone added that way joins the ladder's permanent roster too, not just tonight's game. Removing someone from today's list only takes them out of today; there's no button to erase one player's history from a ladder. To clear everything, Delete the whole ladder — or use Close ladder instead to archive it, which keeps every record but stops it being used for new sessions.

How ratings work

Every player starts at 3.0. After a match, both winners' ratings go up and both losers' go down by the same amount: the margin of victory, times 0.005.

ScoreMarginRating change
11–92±0.010
11–56±0.030
11–29±0.045

It only looks at how big the win was — not who you played. Beating a much stronger or much weaker opponent moves your rating by exactly the same amount.

Recording a match

  • Generate a full session — exactly like the regular scheduler. Every score entered there counts toward ratings automatically.
  • Log a match result — for a game players arranged and played on their own. Pick the players and the score, and it's added straight to the ladder's history.

Standings

View Ladder Standings ranks every player by rating, with their match record and an arrow showing whether their last result moved them up or down.

Ladder Boxes (optional) split a bigger ladder into smaller groups by rating, with players promoted or relegated between boxes at the end of each cycle — useful once a club's ladder gets large.

Tournament

A single event: groups, then a knockout bracket, then a champion.

Pick a competition type

TypeStructure
Standard TournamentGroups play round robin, top finishers go into a seeded knockout bracket.
Club ChampionshipOne combined group, no bracket — best for a single club's own event.
Inter-Club GroupsGroups where every match is against a different club; each club's top pair advances.

Set it up

Add teams — for doubles, you enter both players together as one unit, so there's no separate pairing step. Then set the number of groups, courts available, a rough schedule, and the scoring for group play and for the knockout stage, and tap Generate Groups.

Play it through

  1. Group stage — round-robin matches inside each group, scored right on the match card. A Qualifiers panel shows who's through as results come in.
  2. Start Knockout Stage — once every group match has a score, this builds a seeded bracket automatically. The strongest group results get the easiest early path, the same way a real tournament draw works.
  3. Knockout rounds — winners advance live as scores are entered, through to a Final and a 3rd Place Match, with medals awarded the moment both are decided.
Roster locked at generation. Once a tournament's groups are generated, its roster is fixed — there's no in-app way to add or remove a team. To change who's playing, start a new tournament.

Printing covers Groups, the full Schedule, the Bracket, and just the Medal Games — plus Share, working the same read-only way as a session.

A few other things worth knowing

Saved players

Build your list of regulars once, under Manage saved names, and it's there everywhere — Session Scheduler, Ladder, and Tournament all draw from the same list, so a name only ever needs typing once. Rename someone there and it updates wherever they currently appear.

Install it as an app

On iPhone: tap Share, then Add to Home Screen. On Android or Chrome: use the Install banner the app offers. Either way, it opens full-screen like a real app.
